What the system does

In one sentence: you photograph someone through a window, the glass lays a faint picture of whatever is behind you over the photo, and this system peels that picture off.

From the paper · Figure 1

Try it: wipe the reflection away

Drag the handle across the photo.

What to notice: the green field and trees are not in the room. They are the lawn behind the photographer, reflected in the window glass. The cleaned photo keeps the woman, the bike and the sofa, and the system returns the field as a separate layer.

How a camera turns light into a photo

In one sentence: a camera's raw numbers measure light, and the processing that makes them look good breaks that, so the paper mixes its fake reflections before that point.

Toy example

Try it: walk a photo through the 8 steps

Press Next, or click a step. The picture shows the numbers at that step, put on screen as they are.

What to notice: after step 3 the numbers are proportional to light, so two scenes can be added like light adds on glass. Steps 7 and 8 bend the numbers to look good on a screen, and after that, adding two photos no longer means adding light.

Why the fake reflections have to be mixed as light

In one sentence: glass adds light, so a reflection floods the dark parts of a photo and barely shows on the bright parts, and only mixing in RAW gets that right.

Toy example · built from the paper's Figure 1

Try it: make your own fake reflection

Drag the slider to make the reflected lawn stronger or weaker. Watch spots A and B.

What to notice: adding as light, the lawn floods the dark dress (A) but barely touches the white fur (B), just like the real photo on the right. The finished-photo blend puts the lawn over everything equally and greys out the fur.

How this toy works

The two source pictures are the paper's own outputs for this photo: the clean layer and the reflection layer it separated (Figure 1). Left, they are converted back to approximate linear light and added: \(m = t + k\,r\), with \(k\) set by the slider in stops (each stop doubles or halves the light). Right, the finished images are averaged, \(m = (1-a)\,t + a\,r\), as a simple stand-in for how earlier methods blended 8-bit photos. The real pipeline does the adding in camera XYZ, before white balance, and also models the glass (angle, thickness, blur); see the paper, Sec. 3.
